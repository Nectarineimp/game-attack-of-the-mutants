# Door Smashing & Tactical Combat

## Part 1: Smashing Doors & Barricades

Standard automated science building doors open freely for humans and lock against mutants[cite: 3, 5]. To push inward, mutants must breach doors or human barricades during Phase 2 (or immediately after winning a combat)[cite: 3, 5].

### The Software Glitch Exception
If mutants occupy areas on **both sides** of a door simultaneously, a logic fault in the sensor system causes the door to unlock and open freely[cite: 3, 5]. Mutants may pass through without testing[cite: 3, 5].

### Glow-and-Go Detonation Bonus
If an Extra Glowing (Red) Mutant rolled a `1` during the Glow-and-Go check in that area, it explodes and is removed[cite: 3, 5]. This generates a **free door smash check**[cite: 3].

### The Smash! Matrix
Breaching requires testing with 1D6 based on the number of mutants assaulting the barrier (mutant mobs can be divided to work on different doors from the same room)[cite: 3]:

| Number of Mutants | Roll Needed: Door | Roll Needed: Barricade |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **1** | `6` | *Not Possible* |
| **2** | `5+` | `6` |
| **3** | `4+` | `5+` |
| **4+** | `2+` | `4+` |

* **Success:** Remove the door or barricade counter[cite: 5]. The pathway is permanently open[cite: 5].
* **Failure:** The barrier holds; mutants cannot move through it this phase[cite: 5].
* **Barricade Note:** Barricades are physical blockades constructed by humans[cite: 5]. Humans **cannot** pass through barricades and **cannot** smash them down[cite: 3, 5].

## Part 2: Tactical Combat Resolution

When Mutants and Humans occupy the same room or corridor, combat occurs[cite: 5]. Each engagement is resolved across a maximum of **two rounds**[cite: 3, 5].

### 1. The Tactical Combat Pad Setup
The Combat Pad prevents crowded miniature scrums on the board and structures active lines[cite: 5, 12]:
* **The Battle Line:** A maximum of **3 combatants per side** may fight on the active front line per round[cite: 3]. There must always be at least 1 figure on each side’s front line[cite: 3, 5].
* **Reserves:** Any additional units in the contested area remain off the line in their respective **Reserve Box**[cite: 3, 5, 12].
* **Outnumbering Bonus:** If one side has more units on the active front line than the other (e.g., 3 vs. 1, or 2 vs. 1—not counting reserves), that side gains **+1 Combat Value (CV)** to its rolls[cite: 3, 5].
  * *GunBot Exception:* If a **GunBot™** is on the human front line, the mutants **never** receive the outnumbering bonus[cite: 5].

### 2. Pairings & Combat Rolls
1. **Pairing Off:** The Mutant player chooses which mutant pairs up against which human/bot defender on the line[cite: 3].
2. **Combat Value (CV) & Dice:**
   * Values are written as either flat numbers (e.g., `CV: 4`) or multi-dice codes (e.g., `CV: 3x2`)[cite: 3].
   * An entry of `3x2` means the character rolls **two D6**, takes the single highest die, and adds their base CV[cite: 3].
3. **Roll & Compare:**
   $$\text{Total} = \text{D6 Roll} + \text{Base CV} + \text{Modifiers}$$
   * Both combatants roll simultaneously[cite: 3, 5].
   * **Highest Score Wins:** The loser is eliminated (if a mutant) or placed into the **Wounded Box** (if a human or robot)[cite: 3, 5, 12].
   * **Ties:** Attackers win ties (unless otherwise modified by pre-combat conditions)[cite: 3].

### 3. Special Combat Triggers
* **Wall of Lead (GunBot™):** If a GunBot™ is the **sole defender** in an area, it triggers automatic fire before standard combat begins[cite: 3, 5]. Roll **6 dice (CV: 3x6)**; keep the best three dice and allocate each against up to three attackers[cite: 3, 5]. Leftover dice may be placed against mutant reserves[cite: 5]. After firing, ammo is spent: the bot must be repaired before firing in automatic mode again, but continues fighting in standard combat at `CV: 3x2`[cite: 3, 5]. For tie-breakers, *Wall of Lead* is treated as the attacker[cite: 3].
* **Messy Triangle:** If Penny, Buck, and Percy are all present in the same room, emotional drama causes all three TAs to suffer **-1 CV to defense**[cite: 3].

### 4. End of Round: Recovery of Wounded & Retreats
Between Round 1 and Round 2 (and at the close of Round 2):
* **Recovering Wounded:** Unwounded humans or GripBots™ in the **Reserve Box** or in an **adjacent mutant-free room** may pull wounded figures into an adjacent safe room[cite: 3, 5].
* **Recovery Limits:** One unwounded human or GripBot can only save **one wounded** figure[cite: 3]. If there are more wounded than unwounded rescuers available, excess wounded cannot be saved and are captured/lost permanently[cite: 3].
* **Advancing Reserves:** If casualties cleared slots on the front line, players may advance figures from Reserve onto the line for Round 2[cite: 5].

### 5. Combat Resolution & Pressing Forward
After Round 2 concludes:
1. **Check Annihilation:** If one side is completely defeated (including all reserves), the other side holds the area[cite: 3].
2. **Count Casualties:** If both sides survive, count the wounds/kills generated[cite: 3]. If the attackers inflicted as many or more casualties than the defenders, the attackers win and the defenders must retreat to an adjacent enemy-free room[cite: 3, 5]. If the defenders inflicted more casualties, the attackers retreat[cite: 3].
3. **No Retreat Route:** If retreating units have no valid, mutant-free space to enter, they suffer total defeat[cite: 3]. If a retreat room has insufficient capacity, units that fit survive; the rest are eliminated[cite: 3].
4. **Pressing the Advantage:**
   * **Victorious Mutants:** Immediately gain a free **Smash! action** against an adjoining door or barricade[cite: 3, 5].
   * **Victorious Humans:** May choose to **Press Forward** and immediately move 1 area into an adjacent room (initiating a new combat if mutants are present)[cite: 3, 5].
